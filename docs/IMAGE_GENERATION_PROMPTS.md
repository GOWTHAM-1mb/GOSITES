# Ira Menon Interiors — image generation prompt pack

These prompts are written for the current website, its actual image ratios, and its fictional project copy. They describe proposed fictional spaces. Generated images should be identified on the website as AI-generated concept imagery, not photographs of completed commissions.

The intended look is believable architectural editorial photography: restrained materials, natural daylight and ordinary physical detail. Prompt wording cannot guarantee realism or consistency; inspect the results before choosing final assets.

## How to use this pack

1. Paste the MASTER PHOTOGRAPHY BRIEF below, followed by ONE numbered scene prompt. Generate one image at a time, never a collage or contact sheet.
2. Generate the anchor scene for each project first: N01, B01, R01 and C01. Keep the selected result as that project's visual reference.
3. Attach the selected anchor when generating other views of the same project. Use images from that project only. The reference instructions below assume the relevant image is attached.
4. N02 is an edit/reframe of N01, not an independent new apartment. N03 is a vertical view of the same room.
5. Export at the target ratio and resolution if your generator supports them. Otherwise use its closest available canvas, with enough crop allowance. Do not stretch an image to fit.
6. Keep original high-resolution files. The website will need responsive compressed derivatives, accurate focal points, alt text, and concept-image captions. These prompts do not modify the website or generate the actual assets.

## MASTER PHOTOGRAPHY BRIEF — paste before every scene prompt

```text
Create one exceptionally believable architectural interior photograph of a fictional Indian interior-design concept for an editorial studio website. The visual direction is architectural, tactile, quiet and carefully observed. The image should resemble a real location photograph captured by a professional architectural photographer, with physically plausible construction and everyday material behaviour.

Photographic treatment: a level camera, corrected verticals, natural perspective, controlled exposure and a realistic tonal range. Retain subtle highlight detail outdoors and legible shadow detail indoors without HDR halos or uniformly bright shadows. Use restrained sharpening, delicate natural sensor texture, and realistic depth of field. The main architectural planes should be clear; avoid cinematic background blur. Lens and exposure descriptions in the scene are visual guidance, not text or metadata to print in the image.

Materials must have believable scale and construction: timber grain following the direction of each board, sensible panel joints and edge thicknesses, plaster with restrained variation rather than a distressed texture overlay, stone with subtle mineral structure, linen with natural weave and gravity, cane with a coherent repeating weave. Furniture must stand on the floor, align with the perspective and cast contact shadows. Doors, windows, hinges, frames and thresholds must be buildable. Small irregularities should feel specific and incidental, not repeated procedural patterns.

Styling: a few useful objects placed with restraint. Allow modest signs of ordinary use, such as a softly creased cushion or a book resting slightly off-square. Keep circulation clear. No people, faces, pets, artificial-looking hands or invented portraits. Indian context should come through climate, local materials and spatial character; do not introduce ornamental cultural motifs.

Colour: faithful to the scene's material palette, naturally muted, with restrained greens and timber warmth. Whites must remain neutral to gently warm. Avoid an orange/yellow wash, teal shadows, unnaturally saturated foliage and crushed black shadows. The photograph must work beside an off-white editorial page and dark text without being recoloured to imitate the website.

Deliver one clean edge-to-edge photograph at the requested aspect ratio. No typography, captions, branding, logo, watermark, decorative border, UI mockup or collage. No CGI gloss, plastic materials, waxy surfaces, impossible architecture, fisheye distortion, exaggerated luxury staging, mirrored layouts, duplicated props or conspicuous AI artefacts.
```

## Neem Courtyard Apartment — shared spatial identity

Fictional Bengaluru apartment, not a villa or resort. Approximately 1,850 sq ft. Ceiling approximately 2.9 metres. Warm off-white lime-plaster walls; gently honed grey-green local granite flooring; restrained reclaimed-teak joinery; unbleached linen upholstery. The living room adjoins a small internal courtyard through a teak-framed glazed opening. A modest neem tree grows in an open-to-sky, soil-filled planting bed, with low planting around its base. Avoid making the tree too large for the courtyard. A low linen sofa sits within the living area; a deep window seat and built-in teak bookshelf form a reading corner; an adjacent dining space contains a timber table and six chairs. The same palette, window-frame proportions and furniture language must recur across all views.
